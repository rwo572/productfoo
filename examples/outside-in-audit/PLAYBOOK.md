# Playbook — Outside-in audit landing page (the "we ran foo for you" page)

Status: historical writing playbook from 2026-07-21, retained as an experimental template. It is not a supported automated workflow or permission to contact a recipient. See [scope and setup](README.md).

## Invocation (what the owner provides)

```
audit page:
linkedin: <profile url of the recipient>
company: <name or domain>
angle: <optional steer, e.g. "CEO edition" / "CPO edition" / a thesis to test>
```

Two inputs minimum. Recipient identity MUST be re-verified from a fetched public source (leadership page, dated press) — the LinkedIn URL is an identity *hint*; LinkedIn itself usually blocks fetches. Never trust it alone; never guess a title. Writing direction calibrates to the role: CEO = market/M&A clock + identity moves; CPO = build mechanics, 30-day scopes, first-win framing (a new-in-role CPO needs a first win more than a map).

## Pipeline (phases, each with its gate)

1. **Target deep intake** — Use `bin/foo research "<topic>"` from the repository root (or `foo research "<topic>"` in an installed workspace), follow its plan and source-access handoff, and verify the finished paper with `foo research validate <paper.md>`. Stop and report a planning or validation error rather than altering enforcement state. Five lanes, ≤5 fetches each: (a) product-updates/changelog archive, as far back as pagination allows — report the earliest date reached, never extrapolate; (b) blog/newsroom strategy voice + verbatim self-category language; (c) CEO/exec public voice — verify who the CEO is from a fetched page first; (d) community + third-party reaction (pricing changes, layoffs, migrations); (e) **technical documentation** — the developer/API docs (MCP surface, rate limits, auth, activity/audit primitives, pricing pages). This is where the moves get their real primitives and their real constraints; the feasibility red-team stands on this lane, so a move that dies on a publish throttle or a sandboxed payment lane is caught before it ships. Heavy site-builder marketing sites overflow the harness fetcher: fall back to curl. Declare every unreachable class (Reddit, X) as a gap.
2. **Outsider's read** — one synthesis paragraph (identity change, what funds it, "the words and the shipping match; the tempo doesn't"), plus three takeaway-led quotes forming an arc: *declared → chosen → paid for*. Personalize attributions when the recipient is named ("your blog", "you, on the record"). **FOUNDER/INSIDER RECIPIENT RULE (2026-07-21, from a prototype miss):** when the recipient is a founder or exec who authored the material, NEVER mirror their own story back (their funding history, their homepage copy) — it reads as rehash. The read must contain only what is visible from outside that they cannot see from inside: perception gaps (how the channel/press describes them vs how they describe themselves), absence signals (what they haven't shipped/announced and everyone can see), and the world moving around them. For founder editions, the map IS the lead; the read shrinks to one perception-gap line. **FRESHNESS BAR (same ruling):** target-specific receipts ≤90 days wherever possible; a page dated today cannot lean on year-old links. If nothing fresh exists, the silence is the finding — receipt the absence, not stale news.
3. **The map** — fixed three-slot grammar: (1) incumbents' AI-native transformation, (2) category peers' AI-NATIVE DISTRIBUTION ("partnerships beat features"), (3) AI-native insurgent moves. Every bullet at the bullet-1 bar: named actors + dated money/power verbs + a contrast implicating the reader + one closing law. Heavyweight first-party sources (newsrooms, pricing pages); no changelog inventory, no abstractions as actors, no consultancy blogs. M&A rule: acquirer-side first-party counts; a target's silence about its own acquisition is a signal, not grounds to drop.
4. **Competitor intake** where the map needs receipts — incumbents, category peers, insurgents, hot startups (funding-verified or dropped). Banned idea categories from prior rulings: analytics/dashboards/scanners/reports. **NO CROSS-TARGET RECEIPT PORTING (2026-07-21, from a prototype miss):** map receipts do not transfer between targets even when fresh — the actor set must be re-derived for THIS target's category (a commerce company's incumbents are Shopify/Stripe/OpenAI-checkout/Amazon/Google, not the CMS/DXP stack). Reuse is legitimate only when the actor genuinely sits in the target's map slot; every slot gets a fresh derivation pass per run.
5. **Three moves** — channel-first derivation (what can the target's existing distribution machines sell tomorrow?), FOMO-ranked, each an AI-native lean (transformation, not protection). Ladder when it fits: correct → act → operate. Realism rule: nobody closes a $30k retainer in a chat box — split fixed-price (transact in-conversation) from high-ticket (intake + meeting).
6. **Assembly (data-driven — 2026-07-21).** DO NOT hand-splice HTML (that produced every structural bug: leftover cross-target copy, broken nesting, quote-escaping slips, undefined tokens). Write content as a typed JSON per [SCHEMA.md](SCHEMA.md) and render: `node examples/outside-in-audit/render.mjs <company>.json > foo-audit-<company>.html` (canonical output name, owner convention 2026-07-21). The renderer owns skeleton, `style.css`, `scripts.js` (topo + copy + modal), and escaping (once). Company mark: fetch the target's favicon/webclip PNG, downscale ≤36px, inline as data URI in the `mark` field (referential; page stays foo-branded). Prompts are NATURAL LANGUAGE — concrete fact opener + the real question; `/foo:spec` on moves, `/foo:research` on map bullets. No completed audit is bundled; `SCHEMA.md` is the self-contained data contract.
7. **Gates before showing the owner** — `bin/foo detect --human-surface` on user-facing copy; impeccable design hook clean (em-dash budget ≤2 in body copy; Geist warnings are accepted false positives — committed brand); render-verify in a real browser both halves of the page; every claim's receipt clicks through. **BUILD → SWARM → FIX → SHOW (2026-07-21):** assemble the COMPLETE page first, then run the review swarm on it (scan/design · founder-fit/content · conversion/GTM · receipts/accuracy lenses), fix findings, and only then show the owner. Never drip section-by-section checkpoints — the owner reviews a finished artifact, not a stream of drafts.

## Register (hard-won rules)

Objective over promotional (receipted: skeptics burn effort discounting hype); concise + scannable (bold lead, then support); one CTA goal (attention ratio 1:1) — topbar `Request access` + closing CTA; no machinery words on the surface (swarms, panels, gates, packets); no run-internal jargon or arrow chains; derived numbers only when inputs are verbatim ("arithmetic on public prices, not forecasts"); the artifact's kill condition is ONE wrong claim about the target — when in doubt, cut and disclose the cut.

## Fixed page skeleton (what the template encodes)

topbar (foo wordmark · Request access) → prepared-for line (date · recipient · company mark) → H1 "We ran foo for {Company} — build these next." → lede "Three ways to get an AI-native edge." → The outsider's read on your positioning → The map: who's coming, from where → Steal these AI-native moves (01/02/03, verdict chips, spec prompts) → honest-limits line → What is foo? (three beats: thousand builders/thousand roadmaps · idea to customers in days · take — and make — more shots on goal) → CTA "Go from idea to revenue in 30 days with foo." + Request access + two-command terminal.

## Standing cautions

The page names a real person and carries a real company's mark: it never travels without the owner's explicit send decision. The 30-day claim and any guarantee language need a terms-level home before commercial use. Recipient personalization ("you, on the record") only when the prepared-for name makes it true.
